## T15 — The melody comes back with the reset

**2026-07-27 10:45 PM EDT**

- **Fixed the app going silent forever after the first gate.** `loop()` ran only from `start()`, which is guarded by `isStarted()` and only ever fires on the first CLAIM. The gate called `stopMusic()`, which clears the timer for good, and nothing anywhere restarted it. Every loop from the second onwards had no music at all, which contradicts SPEC §7's level-scaled melody and quietly threw away the best part of the reset: sweet, in-tune, level 1 music returning as though nothing had happened is the joke. T14 made it worse, because the longer pad's own tail became the last music anyone would ever hear.
- **Added `startMusic()` beside `stopMusic()` as its mirror.** Guarded on both sides, `!isStarted() || timer !== null`, so it is safe to call from anywhere and can never stack a second loop on top of a running one, which would have played the melody at double speed. It resets `step` to 0, so loop 1 starts at the top of the phrase rather than halfway through whatever the gate cut off. Added to the returned API next to `stopMusic`.
- **The gate calls it in the cut callback, immediately after `deps.dispatch(reset)`**, so the music comes back with the chaos classes, the mascot and the prize rather than a moment later. The pad's release tail overlapping the restarted melody is intended: at level 1 the tune is sweet and in tune, so it settles into the chord rather than fighting it.
- Wrote the failing tests first, six of them. `audio.test.js` pins the three ways a mirror can be got wrong: not resuming (no notes while stopped, notes again once called), resuming twice (two `startMusic()` calls schedule exactly as many notes as one), and resuming mid-phrase (the restart's first note is the phrase's opening note). Plus the no-op before `start()`, there being no context to schedule against. `gate.test.js` pins the whole audio arc of the scene as an ordered sequence with the reset itself in it, `stopMusic`, `holyPad`, `reset`, `startMusic`, so the restart is placed against the dispatch rather than merely counted. `integration.test.js` gained the one that reproduces the bug end to end: the real engine over a recording `AudioContext`, driven through all eight loops and the gate, then counting notes on loop 1. It failed with `expected 0 to be greater than 0`, which is the silence itself. 271 green.
- **Added `startMusic` to every audio stub in the suite**, in `gate.test.js`, `integration.test.js`, `captcha-shell.test.js`, `captchas-5-8.test.js` and `won.test.js`. Only the first two reach the cut, but a stub that does not match the real interface turns a future change into `undefined is not a function` thrown from inside a timer callback, a long way from its cause.
- Updated SPEC §7: the melody restarts on reset, why, and `startMusic` in the API line, so the next person reading the spec does not reintroduce the bug.
- Verified against the built `dist/index.html` in headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol, with a recording `AudioContext` installed ahead of the bundle so the count is of what the real engine schedules. Played through all eight loops, let the gate cut, and measured a clean window on loop 1: 16 notes in 4 seconds, which is the level 1 tempo of 0.26s per note. Exactly one `AudioContext` for the whole session, before and after the reset, so the context survives and only the loop restarts. The reset itself lands correctly with `chaos=1` and the first prize back.

## T14 — The gate holds its last line

**2026-07-27 10:35 PM EDT**

- **`GATE_TIMING.cut` moved from `6000` to `11000`.** `PLEASE PROCEED TO CLAIM YOUR PRIZE` used to get one second before the hard cut, which is not enough time for the one sincere line in the app to mean anything. It now holds for six. `pad` and `beats` are unchanged: the beats were already well spaced, and all five new seconds belong to the hold.
- **The scene now tells the audio how long it needs.** `gate.js` calls `holyPad((GATE_TIMING.cut - GATE_TIMING.pad) / 1000)`, so the chord length is derived from the timing table rather than duplicated next to it. The table is the only clock in the scene.
- **Gave the pad a sustain stage.** `holyPad()` used `shape()`, a 1.6s attack into a straight decay ending at 6.5s. Called at 1.5s, that chord was gone by 8.0s, so an eleven second scene would have ended in three seconds of dead air over the frozen final line. Added `swell()` beside `shape()` inside `createAudio`, so no new top-level identifier enters the bundle: attack, hold at peak, then release. Per voice that is 0 to 0.15 over 1.6s, flat until 9.5s, out by 10.7s. Full level at the instant of the cut, ringing 1.2s into loop 1, which is less overhang than the old pad already had. `shape()` is untouched: attack-then-decay is right for a note, and blip, buzz and every melody note still use it. The hold is clamped to at least `PAD_ATTACK + 0.1` so the envelope events can never land out of order.
- **Stretched the doors and, more importantly, changed their easing.** The parting was `3.4s cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.3, 1)` with a 1s delay, ending at 5.9s with `forwards` pinning them open, which at the new cut would have been five seconds of a still frame. It is now `10s cubic-bezier(0.6, 0, 0.65, 0.55)`, running 2.5s to 12.5s on the scene clock. The duration alone would not have been enough: the old curve decelerates to a standstill at its end, so merely stretching it satisfies the arithmetic and still hands back a near-frozen final second. The new curve is still travelling at the cut.
- **Kept reduced motion alive.** With the rays and motes at `animation: none` and the doors only materialising, the scene would have sat as a dead frame for nearly five seconds and read as a crash. Replaced the glow's fade-in with a single `gate-glow-hold 10s` opacity animation spanning the scene, rather than layering a second animation on the same property. Nothing moves a pixel, and the 16% knee reproduces the 1.6s fade-in it stands in for. Opacity is not what `prefers-reduced-motion` protects against.
- Wrote the failing tests first. `gate.test.js` gained a pin on the cut itself, which nothing tested before, so it could have moved anywhere and stayed green: the gate is still up with all three beats at `cut - 1` and gone one millisecond later. Plus the six-second hold as a decision rather than a number, and the length the scene asks the audio for. A new `describe('gate.css')` block reads the stylesheet the way `chaos.test.js` and `mascot.test.js` read theirs, summing every duration in a declaration so a reordered shorthand cannot slip through: the doors' delay plus duration exceeds the scene window, and the reduced-motion glow runs at least that long. `audio.test.js` had no coverage of the pad envelope at all, only voice count and oscillator type, so the sustain would have shipped unguarded; it now pins the four envelope events per voice, that the release does not begin before the cut, that every oscillator outlives its own envelope, and that a bare `holyPad()` still covers the scene. Seven failed first. 265 green.
- Updated SPEC §5.3 (roughly 11 seconds, the six-second hold, nothing finishing inside it, the pad sized from the scene clock) and §7 (the pad sustains for a length the gate passes in). Corrected the prose carrying the old clock in `gate.js` and in `gate.css`'s header, door and text sections.
- Verified against the built `dist/index.html` in headless Chrome, driven through all eight loops to the gate over the DevTools protocol. This had to run in real time: Chrome's `--virtual-time-budget` advances timers but freezes the animation timeline, so under it a frozen door and a moving door look identical. In full motion the left door reads 3.3°, 10.2° and 26.0° of rotation at 6s, 8s and 10.5s, so it is still accelerating at the cut rather than settling, and consecutive screenshots differ at every sample. In reduced motion the doors correctly hold still while the glow moves continuously through 0.664, 0.651, 0.914 and 0.937, and consecutive frames still differ. Both modes: all three beats on screen at 10.5s, gone by 11.0s, pristine loop 1 behind it with `chaos=1` and the body class off.

If it booms, the fix is `PAD_GAIN` 0.6 to 0.5 rather than a shorter hold, which would put the dead air back; if the tail reads as a wink, shorten `PAD_RELEASE` rather than ending the release before the cut.
